The Educator

I don't produce grade chasers.
I build thinkers.

I started teaching because I noticed something troubling: students who were told exactly what to write, how to structure it, and what the examiner wanted. They got reasonable grades. But they had no idea why.

That is not education. That is conditioning.

My background is in education, communication, and Cambridge assessment systems. But more than credentials, what drives my teaching is a belief that every student has a genuine perspective worth developing — and that Cambridge, at its best, is actually asking for exactly that.

When a student walks into my studio — whether that is online or in person — I expect them to speak. To disagree. To question. To share half-formed thoughts without embarrassment. That is where real writing begins.

Teaching Philosophy

What I actually believe about learning

01

Thinking before writing

Before a student writes a single word, they must understand what they actually think about the text, the question, the idea. Writing is organised thought. We develop the thought first.

02

Questions are the work

Every question a student asks — no matter how basic — is a sign of a working mind. There is no question I don't want to hear. The student who asks is the student who grows.

03

Hard work is the only path

I don't believe in shortcuts. I believe students are capable of more than they know. My job is to show them that, and then make them prove it — to themselves, not to me.

04

Differentiation is respect

No two students learn the same way. Treating them identically is not fairness — it is laziness. Every student in my studio gets a version of the teaching built for them.

05

Reckless intellectual confidence

Students must learn to share what they think — with full conviction — and then refine it. Timid thinking produces timid writing. I want students who commit to an idea.

06

Cambridge is a thinking test

The 0500 syllabus, done properly, rewards genuine intellectual engagement. Students who understand this stop cramming and start thinking. The grades follow.
